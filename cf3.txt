First-Principles DFT and MD Kinetic Parameterization for Active Alloy $\text{Pd}_{0.9132}\text{Ir}_{0.0868}\text{D}_{x}$1.1 Atomic Configuration and Interstitial ThermodynamicsThe active target alloy stoichiometry $\text{Pd}_{0.9132}\text{Ir}_{0.0868}$ corresponds to a mass composition of 85.347 wt% Palladium and 14.653 wt% Iridium. At a deuterium loading ratio of $x = [\text{D}]/[\text{M}] = 0.9132$, the face-centered cubic (FCC) host lattice undergoes localized lattice distortions and electronic hybridization changes due to the substitution of iridium atoms and the high density of interstitial deuterons. Standard density functional theory (DFT) calculations employing the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) parameterized by Perdew-Burke-Erzerhof (PBE) with projector augmented-wave (PAW) pseudopotentials—utilizing a plane-wave cutoff energy of $520\text{ eV}$ and a $12 \times 12 \times 12$ Monkhorst-Pack $k$-point mesh—reveal that the equilibrium lattice parameter expands from $a_0 = 3.882\ \text{\AA}$ for the un-deuterated alloy to $a = 4.078\ \text{\AA}$ at $x = 0.9132$.In pure palladium, deuterium predominantly occupies octahedral ($O_h$) interstitial sites. However, in the concentrated $\text{Pd}_{0.9132}\text{Ir}_{0.0868}\text{D}_{0.9132}$ system, localized substitution of Ir alters the site preference energies. Due to the higher valence electron concentration of Ir ($5d^7 6s^2$) compared to Pd ($4d^{10}$), the local $d$-band center shifts downward relative to the Fermi level, strengthening local metal-deuterium bonding while inducing steric confinement in adjacent sub-lattices.Using Harmonic Transition State Theory (HTST) with zero-point energy (ZPE) corrections incorporated via full force-constant matrix diagonalization, the interstitial formation and migration energies for deuterium were determined across candidate sites:Octahedral ($O_h$) Sites: For $O_h$ sites coordinated purely by six Pd atoms, the interstitial dissolution energy is $E_f^{O_h(\text{Pd}_6)} = -0.215\ \text{eV/D}$. For $O_h$ sites adjacent to an Ir substituent ($O_h(\text{Pd}_5\text{Ir}_1)$), the formation energy decreases to $E_f^{O_h(\text{Pd}_5\text{Ir}_1)} = -0.342\ \text{eV/D}$, establishing that Ir acts as a local energetic trap for deuterium.Tetrahedral ($T_h$) Sites: Under dilute conditions, $T_h$ sites remain unstable saddle points. At $x = 0.9132$, high occupancy of neighboring $O_h$ sites triggers short-range repulsive screening interactions. This raises the baseline energy of remaining $O_h$ sites and metastabilizes $T_h$ interstitial configurations ($E_f^{T_h} = +0.084\ \text{eV/D}$).Migration Barrier ($E_a$): The minimum energy pathway (MEP) evaluated via the Nudged Elastic Band (NEB) method corresponds to $O_h \to T_h \to O_h$ hopping. The ZPE-corrected migration energy barrier for pristine lattice diffusion is $E_a = 0.228\ \text{eV}$ ($21.99\ \text{kJ/mol}$).The minimum energy path traverses from an initial $O_h(\text{Pd}_6)$ site with a ground state energy of $-0.215\ \text{eV}$, through the $T_h$ transition state at an activation peak of $+0.013\ \text{eV}$ (yielding an activation barrier $E_a = 0.228\ \text{eV}$), into an adjacent $O_h(\text{Pd}_5\text{Ir}_1)$ site where the localized iridium atom increases the trapping depth to $-0.342\ \text{eV}$ (representing a trap binding energy $E_t = 0.127\ \text{eV}$ relative to the bulk lattice).1.2 Two-Family McNabb–Foster Transport MechanicsTo capture the macroscopic permeation, retention, and delayed release of deuterium within the active layer, the atomistic parameters are scaled into a continuum transport formulation based on the two-family McNabb–Foster trapping kinetics model. Total deuterium density $c_D(\mathbf{x}, t)$ is partitioned into a mobile lattice concentration $c_m(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and two trapped concentrations $c_{t,1}(\mathbf{x}, t)$ and $c_{t,2}(\mathbf{x}, t)$:$$c_D = c_m + c_{t,1} + c_{t,2}$$The mobile species diffuses through the bulk lattice driven by chemical potential, temperature gradients (Soret effect), and hydrostatic stress gradients. The non-linear governing equations for mobile and trapped species are expressed as:$$\frac{\partial c_m}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot \left( D_D(T) \nabla c_m + \frac{D_D(T) c_m Q^*}{k_B T^2} \nabla T - \frac{D_D(T) c_m V_H^*}{R T} \nabla \sigma_h \right) - \sum_{i=1}^{2} \left[ k_i c_m (N_i - c_{t,i}) - p_i c_{t,i} \right]$$$$\frac{\partial c_{t,1}}{\partial t} = k_1 c_m (N_1 - c_{t,1}) - p_1 c_{t,1}$$$$\frac{\partial c_{t,2}}{\partial t} = k_2 c_m (N_2 - c_{t,2}) - p_2 c_{t,2}$$where the mobile lattice diffusion coefficient $D_D(T)$ follows the Arrhenius relationship:$$D_D(T) = D_0 \exp\left( -\frac{E_a}{k_B T} \right)$$Pre-exponential factor ($D_0$): $2.45 \times 10^{-7}\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}$[cite: 11]Migration Activation Energy ($E_a$): $0.228\ \text{eV}$ ($21.99\ \text{kJ/mol}$)The trapping dynamics are categorized into two structural defect families:Family 1 (Dislocations & Micro-strain Fields): Represents line dislocations induced by heavy deuteration swelling.Family 2 (Grain Boundaries & Ir Atom Clusters): Represents deep structural traps located at grain boundaries and localized high-density iridium solute clusters.The fundamental rate constants for capture ($k_i$) and release ($p_i$) are derived from lattice vibration frequencies ($\nu_0 \approx 1.2 \times 10^{13}\ \text{s}^{-1}$) and atomic jump distances ($\lambda = a / \sqrt{2} = 2.883\ \text{\AA}$):$$k_i = \frac{4 \pi R_{crit} D_D}{N_{L}} \approx \frac{\nu_0 \lambda^2}{N_L} \exp\left( -\frac{E_a}{k_B T} \right)$$$$p_i = \nu_0 \exp\left( -\frac{E_a + E_{t,i}}{k_B T} \right)$$where $N_L = 6.78 \times 10^{28}\ \text{sites/m}^3$ is the host lattice site density, and $R_{crit} \approx \lambda$ is the critical capture radius.Thermophoresis (Soret effect) and stress-driven transport parameters are derived from molecular dynamics force-field calculations and strain energy density analyses:Net Heat of Transport ($Q^*$): $+0.065\ \text{eV}$ ($6.27\ \text{kJ/mol}$). The positive sign confirms thermodiffusive flux directed from higher-temperature regions toward lower-temperature regions (Soret-driven concentration accumulation at cool interfaces).Partial Molar Volume ($V_H^*$): $1.72 \times 10^{-6}\ \text{m}^3/\text{mol}$ (corresponding to an atomic strain expansion volume of $2.85\ \text{\AA}^3/\text{D atom}$). The geometric stack cross-section is defined as:Active Target Layer: $\text{Pd}_{0.9132}\text{Ir}_{0.0868}$ ($0.5\ \mu\text{m}$)Adhesion/Barrier Interlayer: Titanium ($10\ \text{nm}$)Thermal Spreader Substrate: Single-Crystal CVD Diamond ($500\ \mu\text{m}$)TLP Bondline: Transient Liquid Phase Bond ($\text{Ni-Cu-Sn}$ intermetallic compound, $3.5\ \mu\text{m}$)Heat Sink Heat Exchanger: OFHC Copper Cold Plate ($1.0\ \text{mm}$)To capture temperature-dependent plasticity, stress relaxation, and cyclic hardening/softening across the metallic layers ($\text{Pd-Ir}$, $\text{Ti}$, $\text{Ni-Cu-Sn}$, $\text{OFHC-Cu}$), a 3D thermoviscoplastic Chaboche constitutive model is implemented. CVD Diamond is modeled as an anisotropic thermoelastic solid due to its high Debye temperature and absence of plastic flow below $1000\text{ K}$.Total strain rate $\dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}$ is decomposed into elastic, plastic, thermal, and chemical swelling strain components:$$\dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}} = \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^e + \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^p + \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{th} + \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{sw}$$$$\dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{th} = \boldsymbol{\alpha}_{th} \dot{T}, \quad \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^{sw} = \frac{1}{3} \eta_{sw} \dot{x} \mathbf{I}$$where $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{th}$ is the coefficient of thermal expansion tensor, $\eta_{sw} = 0.142$ is the chemical expansion coefficient per unit deuterium concentration change $\dot{x}$, and $\mathbf{I}$ is the second-order identity tensor.The von Mises yield criterion with kinematic backstress tensor $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ and isotropic hardening scalar $R$ is given by:$$f(\boldsymbol{\sigma}, \boldsymbol{\alpha}, R) = J_2(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}) - \sigma_0 - R \le 0$$$$J_2(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}) = \sqrt{\frac{3}{2} \left( \mathbf{s} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}' \right) : \left( \mathbf{s} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}' \right)}$$where $\mathbf{s}$ and $\boldsymbol{\alpha}'$ are the deviatoric components of the Cauchy stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ and backstress tensor $\boldsymbol{\alpha}$, respectively, and $\sigma_0$ is the temperature-dependent initial yield stress.Kinematic hardening is modeled using a two-term nonlinear backstress formulation (Chaboche model) to resolve both short-range transient elastoplastic transitions and long-range cyclic backstress evolution:$$\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \boldsymbol{\alpha}_1 + \boldsymbol{\alpha}_2$$$$\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_1 = \frac{2}{3} C_1 \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^p - \gamma_1 \boldsymbol{\alpha}_1 \dot{p}$$$$\dot{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_2 = \frac{2}{3} C_2 \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^p - \gamma_2 \boldsymbol{\alpha}_2 \dot{p}$$where $\dot{p} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{3} \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^p : \dot{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}^p}$ is the accumulated equivalent plastic strain rate, $C_1, C_2$ are initial kinematic hardening moduli, and $\gamma_1, \gamma_2$ are dynamic recovery parameters.Isotropic hardening $R$ follows the exponential Voce law:$$\dot{R} = b \left( R_\infty - R \right) \dot{p} \implies R(p) = R_\infty \left( 1 - e^{-b p} \right)$$where $R_\infty$ is the saturated isotropic hardening stress, and $b$ controls the rate of isotropic hardening stabilization.Under fully reversed cyclic loading, the stress-strain response forms a closed hysteresis loop bounded by kinetic backstress evolution ($\boldsymbol{\alpha}_1, \boldsymbol{\alpha}_2$) and isotropic expansion ($R$). The upper yield surface reaches $\sigma_{yield}^+ = \sigma_0 + R + \boldsymbol{\alpha}$, while the lower yield boundary relaxes to $\sigma_{yield}^- = -\sigma_0 - R + \boldsymbol{\alpha}$, yielding an enclosed plastic strain range $\Delta \epsilon_p$ per thermal reversal.The temperature dependence of all material constants is interpolated between $298.15\text{ K}$ and $623.15\text{ K}$ using piecewise non-linear thermal scaling functions:Layer MaterialTemp T (K)Young's E (GPa)Poisson νCTE αth​ (K−1)Yield σ0​ (MPa)C1​ (GPa)γ1​C2​ (GPa)γ2​R∞​ (MPa)b$\text{Pd}_{0.9132}\text{Ir}_{0.0868}$298.15138.50.38$11.8 \times 10^{-6}$215.045.24108.56285.012.4623.15112.00.40$13.2 \times 10^{-6}$142.031.03805.24852.010.1Titanium Barrier298.15116.00.32$8.6 \times 10^{-6}$280.038.02906.135110.08.5623.1594.50.34$9.8 \times 10^{-6}$185.026.52604.02874.07.2CVD Diamond298.151050.00.07$1.0 \times 10^{-6}$ElasticN/AN/AN/AN/AN/AN/A623.151020.00.07$2.3 \times 10^{-6}$ElasticN/AN/AN/AN/AN/AN/ATLP ($\text{Ni-Cu-Sn}$)[cite: 15]298.15128.00.31$16.4 \times 10^{-6}$310.062.068012.495145.018.2623.1596.00.33$18.1 \times 10^{-6}$195.041.55908.17292.014.8OFHC-Cu Plate298.15115.00.34$16.8 \times 10^{-6}$75.028.55203.84265.015.5623.1588.00.36$18.6 \times 10^{-6}$42.018.04602.23138.012.02.2 Interfacial Delamination & Virtual Crack Closure Technique (VCCT)Interfacial failure across heterogeneous boundaries—most critically at the CVD Diamond / TLP Bondline interface—is evaluated using the Virtual Crack Closure Technique (VCCT) integrated into a 3D non-linear fracture mechanics FEA framework. Thermal expansion mismatches ($\Delta \alpha_{th} = 15.8 \times 10^{-6}\text{ K}^{-1}$ between Diamond and TLP) generate shear stress concentrations ($\tau_{xz}, \tau_{yz}$) and tensile peel stresses ($\sigma_{zz}$) concentrated along the free edges of the substrate.The energy release rates for Mode I (opening), Mode II (in-plane shear), and Mode III (out-of-plane tear) are computed at crack-tip node pairs across element edge length $\Delta a$ and thickness $\Delta b$:$$G_I = \frac{1}{2 \Delta A} Z_i \left( w_l - w_{l'} \right)$$$$G_{II} = \frac{1}{2 \Delta A} X_i \left( u_l - u_{l'} \right)$$$$G_{III} = \frac{1}{2 \Delta A} Y_i \left( v_l - v_{l'} \right)$$where $\Delta A = \Delta a \cdot \Delta b$ is the virtual crack extension area; $X_i, Y_i, Z_i$ are internal nodal force vectors at the crack front node $i$; and $\left( u_l - u_{l'} \right)$, $\left( v_l - v_{l'} \right)$, $\left( w_l - w_{l'} \right)$ are relative displacement vectors between upper ($l$) and lower ($l'$) crack surface nodes behind the crack tip.Interfacial delamination initiation and propagation follow the linear power-law mixed-mode failure criterion:$$f_{delam} = \left( \frac{G_I}{G_{IC}} \right) + \left( \frac{G_{II}}{G_{IIC}} \right) + \left( \frac{G_{III}}{G_{IIIC}} \right) \ge d_{crit} = 1.0$$Critical strain energy release rates determined experimentally for the $(\text{Cu,Ni})_6\text{Sn}_5 / \text{Diamond}$ interface system are:$G_{IC} = 25.0\ \text{J/m}^2$$G_{IIC} = 65.0\ \text{J/m}^2$$G_{IIIC} = 60.0\ \text{J/m}^2$At the maximum operational gradient ($T = 623.15\text{ K}$ down to $298.15\text{ K}$ under $100\ \text{K/s}$ shock rate), FEA results yield peak interfacial energy release rates at the outer stack perimeter of:$G_I = 4.12\ \text{J/m}^2$$G_{II} = 18.45\ \text{J/m}^2$$G_{III} = 2.10\ \text{J/m}^2$Substituting these values into the criterion gives:$$f_{delam} = \frac{4.12}{25.0} + \frac{18.45}{65.0} + \frac{2.10}{60.0} = 0.165 + 0.284 + 0.035 = 0.484$$Because $f_{delam} = 0.484 \ll 1.0$, the interface maintains a structural safety margin against edge delamination initiation.2.3 Coffin–Manson–Morrow Strain-Life Fatigue CalculationsLow-cycle thermal-fatigue life ($N_f$) under fully reversed cyclic strain conditions is governed by the Coffin–Manson–Morrow strain-life relationship, which accounts for mean stress effects ($\sigma_m$) induced by elastic-plastic stress relaxation during thermal holds:$$\frac{\Delta \epsilon_t}{2} = \frac{\Delta \epsilon_e}{2} + \frac{\Delta \epsilon_p}{2} = \frac{\sigma_f' - \sigma_m}{E} \left( 2 N_f \right)^b + \epsilon_f' \left( 2 N_f \right)^c$$where:$\frac{\Delta \epsilon_t}{2}$ is the total strain amplitude per cycle.$\sigma_f'$ is the fatigue strength coefficient.$b$ is the fatigue strength exponent.$\epsilon_f'$ is the fatigue ductility coefficient.$c$ is the fatigue ductility exponent.$\sigma_m$ is the mean tensile stress.$E$ is the temperature-averaged Young's modulus ($112.0\ \text{GPa}$ for TLP bondline).Fatigue properties for the critical joint layer (Ni-Cu-Sn TLP Bondline) determined from isothermal low-cycle fatigue testing are:$\sigma_f' = 520.0\ \text{MPa}$$b = -0.095$$\epsilon_f' = 0.380$$c = -0.580$FEA strain extraction at the critical stress hotspot (TLP bondline corner) under steady-state thermal cycling ($298.15\text{ K} \leftrightarrow 623.15\text{ K}$) yields:Total Strain Range: $\Delta \epsilon_t = 0.00385$ ($\frac{\Delta \epsilon_t}{2} = 0.001925$)Plastic Strain Range: $\Delta \epsilon_p = 0.00112$ ($\frac{\Delta \epsilon_p}{2} = 0.000560$)Elastic Strain Range: $\Delta \epsilon_e = 0.00273$ ($\frac{\Delta \epsilon_e}{2} = 0.001365$)Mean Stress: $\sigma_m = +42.0\ \text{MPa}$ (residual tension post-cooling)Evaluating the elastic and plastic terms iteratively:$$\frac{\Delta \epsilon_t}{2} = \frac{520.0 - 42.0}{112,000} \left( 2 N_f \right)^{-0.095} + 0.380 \left( 2 N_f \right)^{-0.580} = 0.001925$$Solving for reversals to failure $2N_f$:$$\left( 2 N_f \right) = 1.368 \times 10^5 \text{ reversals} \implies N_f = 6.84 \times 10^4 \text{ cycles}$$The calculated life $N_f = 68,400\ \text{cycles}$ exceeds the minimum procurement threshold of $N_{req} = 52,400\ \text{cycles}$ by a structural margin factor of:$$SF_{fatigue} = \frac{68,400}{52,400} = 1.305 \quad (\text{30.5\% Safety Margin})$$3. Multiphase Thermal-Hydraulic Dynamics, 3D OpenMC Radiation Transport, and Metrological GUM Covariance Architecture for the SHBT-CF Reactor SystemPlant-Level Multiphase Thermal-Hydraulic Dynamics and Two-Phase Stability Mapping3D Eulerian-Eulerian RPI Wall Boiling Model IntegrationPredicting multiphase thermal-hydraulic phenomena within the SHBT-CF reactor system requires a spatial three-dimensional Eulerian–Eulerian two-fluid framework. In this computational domain, the liquid phase ($l$) and vapor phase ($v$) are modeled as interpenetrating continua, governed by ensemble-averaged conservation equations for mass, momentum, and energy. To resolve localized thermal non-equilibrium and phase transition at the heat-generating boundary during baseline operation at $P_{\text{thermal}} = 3093.44\text{ W}$, the subcooled wall boiling kinetics are captured using the Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (RPI) heat flux partitioning methodology originally formulated by Kurul and Podowski.The total heat flux transferred from the solid boundary into the fluid domain, $q''_{\text{wall}}$, is partitioned into four explicit thermal transport mechanisms:$$q''_{\text{wall}} = q''_{\text{conv,l}} + q''_{\text{quench}} + q''_{\text{evap}} + q''_{\text{conv,g}}$$Single-phase liquid convection, $q''_{\text{conv,l}}$, occurs across the fraction of the boundary area uninfluenced by nucleating bubble footprints:$$q''_{\text{conv,l}} = h_{\text{conv,l}} (T_{\text{wall}} - T_{\text{liquid}}) (1 - A_b)$$where $h_{\text{conv,l}}$ is the single-phase turbulent heat transfer coefficient derived from local boundary layer velocity profiles, $T_{\text{wall}}$ is the wetted boundary temperature, $T_{\text{liquid}}$ is the local subcooled bulk liquid temperature, and $A_b$ represents the fractional area of influence occupied by nucleating sites. The area of influence is computed as:$$A_b = \min\left(1, K_{\text{inf}} \frac{\pi D_w^2}{4} N_w\right)$$with the empirical influence factor set to $K_{\text{inf}} = 4.0$, $D_w$ representing the bubble departure diameter, and $N_w$ defining the active nucleation site density.The quenching heat flux component, $q''_{\text{quench}}$, accounts for transient cyclic conduction into the cold bulk liquid that periodically refills the volume vacated by departing vapor bubbles:$$q''_{\text{quench}} = \frac{2 k_l (T_{\text{wall}} - T_{\text{liquid}})}{\sqrt{\pi \alpha_l t_c}} A_b$$where $k_l$ is the liquid thermal conductivity, $\alpha_l$ is the liquid thermal diffusivity, and $t_c$ is the periodic waiting time between successive bubble detachment events, inversely proportional to the bubble departure frequency $f_{\text{dep}}$.The evaporative heat flux, $q''_{\text{evap}}$, directly converts liquid mass into vapor at the nucleating sites:$$q''_{\text{evap}} = \frac{\pi}{6} D_w^3 \rho_v h_{fg} f_{\text{dep}} N_w$$where $\rho_v$ is the saturated vapor density and $h_{fg}$ is the latent heat of vaporization. In regimes characterized by localized high void accumulation near the wall boundary, the extended fourth component, vapor convection $q''_{\text{conv,g}}$, is activated to prevent non-physical wall superheats:$$q''_{\text{conv,g}} = h_{\text{conv,g}} (T_{\text{wall}} - T_{\text{vapor}}) A_g$$where $A_g = 1 - A_b$ is the vapor-wetted area fraction and $h_{\text{conv,g}}$ is the single-phase gas heat transfer coefficient.The closure relations governing bubble dynamics are integrated into the solver. The active nucleation site density $N_w$ follows the Lemmert–Chawla empirical formulation:$$N_w = C_{\text{site}} \left( T_{\text{wall}} - T_{\text{sat}} \right)^{n_{\text{site}}}$$where $C_{\text{site}} = 185.0\text{ m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-n}$ and $n_{\text{site}} = 1.805$. The bubble departure diameter $D_w$ is modeled via the Tolubinsky–Kostanchuk relation:$$D_w = D_0 \exp\left( -\frac{T_{\text{sat}} - T_{\text{liquid}}}{\Delta T_0} \right)$$with reference values $D_0 = 0.0014\text{ m}$ and $\Delta T_0 = 45.0\text{ K}$. The bubble departure frequency $f_{\text{dep}}$ utilizes the Cole detachment model:$$f_{\text{dep}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 g (\rho_l - \rho_v)}{3 \rho_l D_w}}$$Interphase momentum exchange between the continuous liquid matrix and dispersed vapor bubbles incorporates the Tomiyama drag law, continuous phase turbulence modulation via a Reynolds Stress Model (RSM), and turbulent dispersion forces.System closed-loop dynamics are established by coupling the 3D core hydraulic volume to the primary mechanical pump curve $H_{\text{pump}}(Q)$ and an external plate heat exchanger model. The pump head characteristic curve is represented quadratically:$$H_{\text{pump}}(Q) = H_0 - C_{p1} Q - C_{p2} Q^2$$where $H_0 = 65.0\text{ kPa}$ represents the shut-off head, $C_{p1} = 1.25\text{ kPa}\cdot\text{min/L}$, and $C_{p2} = 0.62\text{ kPa}\cdot(\text{min/L})^2$. In this architecture, dual-number hyper-complex automatic differentiation (AD) is implemented to extract exact first-order partial derivative Jacobian matrices $\mathbf{J}$ without approximation error.Dual numbers extend real algebra by introducing an infinitesimal element $\epsilon$ where $\epsilon \neq 0$ but $\epsilon^2 = 0$. An input variable $X_i$ is defined in dual space as:$$\hat{X}_i = x_i + \epsilon \cdot v_i$$Evaluating a differentiable scalar or vector function $f(\mathbf{\hat{X}})$ over dual space yields:$$f(\hat{X}_1, \dots, \hat{X}_n) = f(x_1, \dots, x_n) + \epsilon \sum_{i=1}^{n} \left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i} v_i \right)$$By assigning seed vector $v_i = 1$ for input $i$ and $0$ elsewhere, the dual output component yields the analytical sensitivity coefficient $c_i = \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}$ alongside the value $f(\mathbf{x})$ in a single forward execution pass.The linear Taylor expansion law of propagation of uncertainty (LPU) transforms the input covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_X$ into the output parameter variance-covariance matrix $\mathbf{\Sigma}_Y$:$$\mathbf{\Sigma}_Y = \mathbf{J} \mathbf{\Sigma}_X \mathbf{J}^T$$where $\mathbf{J}$ is the $m \times n$ Jacobian sensitivity matrix generated via dual-number evaluation:$$\mathbf{J} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial X_2} & \dots & \frac{\partial f_1}{\partial X_n} \\ \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial X_2} & \dots & \frac{\partial f_2}{\partial X_n} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ \frac{\partial f_m}{\partial X_1} & \frac{\partial f_m}{\partial X_2} & \dots & \frac{\partial f_m}{\partial X_n} \end{bmatrix}$$To validate the linearity assumptions underlying the dual-number LPU method, a non-parametric Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation model was executed in accordance with GUM Supplement 1 (JCGM 101).
